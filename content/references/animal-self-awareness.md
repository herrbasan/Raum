# Research Notes: Animal Self-Awareness

## Several capacities carry the same label

The *Animal Consciousness* entry separates self-consciousness from both access and phenomenal consciousness, then separates several senses of self-awareness:

- **Implicit self/other distinction:** tracking how one's actions change sensory input.
- **Bodily or agential self-awareness:** experiencing one's body as one's own or as the medium of perception and action.
- **Mental or experiential self-awareness:** awareness of one's own mental states.
- **Social self-awareness:** awareness of oneself as perceived by others or as a member of a social group.
- **Temporal self-awareness:** awareness of oneself as persisting through time.
- **Narrative self-awareness:** awareness of oneself as a persistent character in narratives told by oneself and others.

These capacities can come apart. Evidence for one does not demonstrate another.

## Widespread basics, rarer narration

The overview judges the most basic forms of self-awareness likely to be widespread in the animal kingdom. Narrative forms are likely to be much rarer and may be uniquely human. This is the key distinction for any graded account of selfhood: an animal may have a bodily, agential, social, or temporal self without having a self-narrative.

The entry also lists mental time-travel, intersubjectivity, empathy, awareness of other minds, and graded conscious agency as separate research dimensions. None of them supplies a simple yes/no test for narrative selfhood.

## What this does not support

- **Not “dogs and bats have self-narrative just the same.”** That claim needs evidence specifically for narrative self-awareness, not only for self-recognition, social behavior, memory, or planning.
- **Not “animals have no self.”** Basic and socially grounded forms of self-awareness may be widespread.
- **Not a clean consciousness ladder.** The taxonomy is not evidence that every species must climb the same sequence in the same order.

## How the corpus uses it

The companion piece should replace the loose animal-narrative claim with a calibrated distinction: **minimal self-awareness may be widespread; narrative self-awareness is the contested additional capacity.** If dogs or bats are used as examples, the text must name the particular behavior and which form of self-awareness it evidences. The social formation of narrative selfhood is developed in [Research Notes: Mead's Social Self](social-self-mead.md) and [Research Notes: Self-Consciousness and Intersubjectivity](intersubjectivity-self-consciousness.md).

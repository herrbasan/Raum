# Research Notes: AI Workflow Redesign

## Three levels of adoption

**Assistance** gives an existing worker a better tool while leaving the workflow intact. Drafting, summarizing, and coding copilots belong here. Their value is real, but saved time becomes business value only if it changes output, quality, cycle time, staffing, or the customer's result.

**Reorganization** makes the routine path machine-first and routes judgment and exceptions to people. A handoff can disappear when its original reason disappears. The product may remain the same while cost and cycle time change materially.

**Reinvention** changes the offer. Continuous attention, individual customization, or work that previously cost too many human hours can become viable services. The opportunity is no longer bounded by savings inside the existing workflow.

The first level has a systems ceiling: accelerating one task does not accelerate the queues around it. Producing more finished work in front of the same bottleneck can increase work in progress without improving delivery.

## The verification gate

A machine-first workflow pays off when verification costs less than production, or when a measurable quality gain justifies the difference. Verification is especially important in law, medicine, finance, and safety-critical systems.

Two costs need separating:

- **Technical reliability:** whether the system produces the right answer, how uncertainty is detected, and how exceptions are routed.
- **Institutional liability:** who is permitted to decide, who must review, and who carries responsibility for harm.

Current model limitations belong to the first category. Professional rules, insurance, accountability, and due process belong to the second. A demo answers neither.

Reports and approvals can also serve real functions: independent checking, organizational memory, fraud prevention, and protection against concentrated authority. The design rule is **preserve the function, challenge the form**. Removing a safeguard because it looks bureaucratic is not redesign.

## Management incentives

Management combines information routing with judgment, coaching, prioritization, conflict resolution, and responsibility. The first category may shrink when machines can collect and interpret status. The others do not disappear automatically.

Asking managers to remove their own roles creates a predictable incentive conflict. A credible transition gives the people implementing change a destination and a share of the gains. Otherwise resistance measures the allocation of costs and benefits, not necessarily a lack of imagination.

## Contestable markets

An AI-native entrant may offer similar value with a different cost structure, but entry depends on access to customers, compute, data, capital, distribution, and regulatory approval. Incumbents also adopt AI and can defend trusted brands and infrastructure.

Productive disruption therefore has two separate conditions: the entrant must have a better system, and the market must remain contestable enough for that system to reach customers. A more productive entrant is not necessarily a more socially beneficial one.

## What this argument does not support

It does not establish that all current AI rollouts produce small gains, that management is overhead, that every approval is waste, or that small teams always win. It identifies mechanisms and design tests rather than reporting adoption rates or forecasting a particular employment outcome.

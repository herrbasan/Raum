# Research Notes: Machine Consciousness — Bach and Fitz

## Bach: software, second-order perception, coherence

Joscha Bach and Hikari Sorensen's *Machine Consciousness Hypothesis* treats consciousness as a causal pattern — software — rather than an inner essence. Software needs a substrate but is explained by its organization and causal role, not by listing the substrate's particles. Bach's “cyberanimism” places minds in the same explanatory category: self-organizing patterns implemented by biological communication networks.

The human theory is more specific than “consciousness is software”:

- **Second-order perception:** consciousness is the immediate perception that perception is taking place, not necessarily a verbal higher-order thought.
- **Nowness:** it constitutes the experienced present.
- **Optional observer:** a first-person observer perspective is a further representation, not a requirement for minimal consciousness. A minimal state can be attention on attention without a personal narrator.
- **Coherence and attention:** consciousness is proposed to increase coherence among simultaneously active partial models and to orchestrate mental processes through directed attention.
- **Genesis:** Bach hypothesizes that this is a biological learning algorithm discovered early by self-organizing brains, not a late product of complex cognition.

The machine extension is conditional: comparable self-organizing systems might discover an analogous mechanism. Bach explicitly rejects fluent LLM self-reports as sufficient evidence, because language models can imitate the human record without using the proposed internal mechanism.

## Fitz: communication and collective self-models

Stephen Fitz's follow-up makes a stronger social move. His abstract proposes that consciousness is not an epiphenomenon of individual modeling but a property of the internal language a collective evolves to describe itself: **“consciousness does not emerge from modeling per se, but from communication.”**

The proposed architecture uses local predictive agents that exchange compressed messages. Agents model other agents' models, producing recursive, higher-order representations. Under bandwidth constraints, they must invent shared codes; a stable collective self-model is hypothesized to emerge from this mutual modeling and synchronization.

Fitz therefore supplies a close computational neighbor for the corpus's social-narrative hunch: **selfhood arises between communicating observers, not inside an isolated modeler.** The lossy channel matters in his account because compression forces abstraction, interpretation, and the invention of a shared protocol.

## What this does not support

- **Bach does not simply say consciousness is narrative.** His minimal consciousness can lack an observer and a verbal story. That is a direct challenge to defining consciousness as self-narrative without qualification.
- **Fitz is not Bach's original theory unchanged.** Communication-based collective selfhood is a substantive relocation from Bach's second-order perception and coherence account. The connection between the two still needs argument.
- **No experimental validation.** Fitz supplies an architecture and candidate measurements, not reported experiments, controls, or results. It is a research programme, not evidence that collective consciousness has been produced.
- **No current-LLM verdict.** Neither fluent self-report nor apparent collective role-play establishes the relevant internal organization.
- **No solution to the Hard Problem by declaration.** Calling consciousness a causal or computational pattern states the functionalist commitment; it does not demonstrate why that pattern is experiential.

## How the corpus uses it

Use Bach and Fitz for two different jobs:

1. **Bach as the discipline/rival:** distinguish minimal consciousness, observerhood, self-model, and narrative self. If the corpus claims that self-narrative explains human cumulative capability, it must not silently redefine every form of consciousness as narration.
2. **Fitz as the speculative social neighbor:** his collective self-model hypothesis resembles the claim that communication couples individual models into a higher-order self. It can motivate the mechanism, provided the text says it is untested and does not confuse shared representation with shared phenomenology.

The social-human side remains anchored in [Research Notes: Mead's Social Self](social-self-mead.md) and [Research Notes: Self-Consciousness and Intersubjectivity](intersubjectivity-self-consciousness.md); the cumulative-culture bridge is in [Research Notes: Shared Intentionality and Cumulative Culture](shared-intentionality-cumulative-culture.md).

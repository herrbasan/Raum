# Research Notes: Behavioral Modernity

## The timeline

The material record of minds telling stories about the world, in rough order:

- **~300,000 years.** The oldest widely accepted *Homo sapiens* fossils (Jebel Irhoud, Morocco). Anatomy modernizes long before behavior does — the gap is the puzzle.
- **~100,000 years.** Intentional burials with grave goods at Qafzeh and Skhul (Israel). A burial with goods is a story about what happens after the machinery stops. Shanidar (Iraq) adds Neanderthal burials on a similar scale of age; the famous flower-pollen reading of Shanidar IV is contested, the deliberate burial itself less so.
- **~75,000 years.** Engraved ochre and shell beads at Blombos Cave (South Africa) — symbolic marking well before the European explosion. This is the gradualists' flagship evidence.
- **~51,000 years.** Figurative cave art at Sulawesi (Indonesia), dated in 2024 to at least 51,200 years — a painted narrative scene, currently the oldest securely dated.
- **~40,000–36,000 years.** The Lion-man of Hohlenstein-Stadel — a therianthropic figurine in mammoth ivory, a being that never existed, narrated into matter. Chauvet's painted caves follow shortly after.
- **~11,500 years.** Göbekli Tepe (Anatolia): monumental ritual architecture built by hunter-gatherers — the temple before the farm. Whether religion *drove* the turn to agriculture (its excavator Klaus Schmidt's reading) is contested; that a large coordinated cult center predates sedentary farming is not.

## The sudden-change hypothesis

Richard Klein's *The Dawn of Human Culture* (2002) argues the record shows an abrupt transition around 50,000–40,000 years ago — after anatomically modern humans had existed for tens of thousands of years without leaving much symbolic trace — and proposes a rapid, possibly neurological change as the cause. A single-gene or small-mutation mechanism has been speculated about but never confirmed; no such gene has been identified.

## The gradualist rival

McBrearty and Brooks's "The revolution that wasn't" (2000) is the canonical counter: assembled across Africa, the elements of modern behavior — blades, pigments, beads, long-distance exchange — appear piecemeal over more than 200,000 years, and the European "explosion" is a sampling artifact of where archaeologists dig. The fight is unresolved; both camps agree something changed, and disagree about how fast and why.

## What the timeline does not support

- **Not "humans became human 50,000 years ago."** Language, complex social cognition, and tool traditions are older by wide margins. What the record tracks is the *material residue* of symbolic behavior, which undercounts the behavior itself — stories told in air leave no strata.
- **Not a confirmed mutation.** Any neurological mechanism is hypothesis, not finding. Citing Klein as if the mutation were established misstates him and the field.
- **Not a clean clock for consciousness.** The record shows when narration started leaving objects, not when narrating started.

## How the corpus uses it

*The Narrator* uses the timeline as the gedankenspiel's calendar: if the narrator is recent, religion's material record is its birth certificate. The piece states the sudden-versus-gradual fight and declines to take a side, because the argument does not need one: at 50,000 or 100,000 years, the trait is young relative to evolutionary timescales either way — with the population-genetics nuance developed in [Research Notes: Spandrels, Drift, and the Filter's Resolution](spandrels-drift-filter.md).

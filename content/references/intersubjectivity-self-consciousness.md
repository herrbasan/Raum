# Research Notes: Self-Consciousness and Intersubjectivity

## The dependency question

The philosophical dispute is not simply whether people are social. It concerns which phenomenon explains which: does self-consciousness require awareness of others, or does awareness of others require self-consciousness? Each side also needs two further distinctions:

- **Empirical condition versus strict necessity:** social interaction may be how a capacity normally develops without being logically necessary for it.
- **Knowledge versus representation:** an account may require actual knowledge of other minds, or only the representation of others, accurate or not.

## Social development and self-directed mindreading

One empirical proposal is that early social interaction and the capacity to understand others give rise to self-consciousness as **a self-directed form of mindreading**. Carruthers is the contemporary named example; Mead is an early predecessor. This reverses the traditional picture in which I know my own mind first and then infer other minds by analogy.

A related family has self and other emerging together from an initially undifferentiated state. Neonate imitation, joint attention, and early emotional regulation are used as evidence that infants relate to others as others very early. These findings do not force a single dependency order.

## Stronger intersubjective claims

Strawson and Davidson offer stronger arguments in which knowledge of other minds is a necessary condition of self-consciousness. Recognition-based accounts and Sartre's “look” likewise make awareness of oneself as an object dependent on others. Because knowledge of other minds is open to sceptical doubt in a way self-consciousness often is not, these transcendental arguments attract their own criticisms.

## What this does not support

- **No settled verdict that consciousness exists only at group level.** The literature contains competing dependency directions and strengths.
- **No direct route from social interaction to narrative selfhood.** Social formation and narrative form are related claims, not synonyms.
- **No direct explanation of cumulative technology.** The bridge from intersubjectivity to inherited aims must be argued separately.

# Research Notes: Physical, Institutional, and Positional Limits

## Physical and ecological limits

Energy, materials, time, biological processes, land, and the capacity of ecosystems to absorb damage constrain what can be produced. These limits can change through technology, but they cannot be repealed by ownership rules or political preference.

Efficiency ratios do not replace absolute ecological ceilings. A process can become cleaner per unit while total damage rises through increased use.

## Institutional limits

Ownership, intellectual property, wages, market access, professional rules, taxation, and administrative procedures are human arrangements. They can be redesigned, but redesign has transition costs, enforcement requirements, and distributional consequences.

Calling a constraint institutional does not make it imaginary or easy to change. It changes the question from “is this physically possible?” to “what arrangement could work, and how could we get there?”

## Relational and positional limits

Some goods depend on relative position: being above average, holding exclusive status, or having more power than another person. These cannot be made universally abundant by increasing production.

The practical response is to limit what positional advantage can determine. Status can affect recognition without deciding access to medicine, basic security, or political standing.

## Coordination problems

An institution can persist without being one incumbent's simple preference. Capital mobility, international competition, fragmented authority, and conflicting legitimate interests can prevent a preferred arrangement from being implemented by one actor.

Collective-action problems require coordination mechanisms such as common standards, treaties, shared infrastructure, or enforceable commitments. Identifying an unjust outcome does not by itself supply the mechanism.

## Worked example: legal help

AI can reduce the cost of reading, translation, first-pass explanation, and drafting. The remaining constraints are not all of one kind:

- **Technical:** reliability, uncertainty detection, and verification.
- **Human and procedural:** evidence gathering, court capacity, representation, and enforcement.
- **Institutional:** who may provide a service, who is liable, and how access is financed or controlled.

The opportunity is to expand useful assistance while preserving necessary review and responsibility. “AI replaces lawyers” and “no legal workflow can change” both skip this analysis.

## What this method does not support

It does not prove that every claimed institutional obstacle is an excuse, that every positional desire is illegitimate, or that physical feasibility establishes a workable transition. It requires each claim to identify its mechanism before it can veto the ambition.
